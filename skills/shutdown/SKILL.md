# Shutdown

Run the shutdown playbook. Read these first, then follow the playbook's numbered steps in order:

1. `../flow/SKILL.md` (paths are relative to this skill's own directory) — the way, and the draft-confirm-write posture
2. `../flow/references/shutdown.md` — the playbook itself
3. `../flow/references/mcp-tools.md` — tool usage and the gotchas that bite

Seven things that go wrong most often, so hold them in mind from the start:

- **Offer the app's Shutdown ritual first — and you can start it.** A task titled `"Do shutdown 15m"` plus a timer opens the real ritual in the app. It's the fuller version; this door is the short alternative for someone not at the app or who'd rather close out here.
- **Never say their Shutdown ritual is complete.** It isn't. What you write is a journal reflection *alongside* theirs, and claiming otherwise is the one thing that would make this door untrustworthy.
- **Ask how their day was before you look anything up.** Leading with their completed tasks anchors the reflection to that list and you lose the part no query returns. If they've got nothing to give, don't push — show the record and let it become the prompt.
- **Stop any running timer before anything else** — quietly, not as the conversation. A timer left going overnight distorts the numbers the weekly WRAP treats as ground truth.
- **It's not inbox zero — it's nothing unplanned with a hook in it.** The core move is asking what they'd still be thinking about at nine tonight, and giving each of those a plan. The plan is what releases it, not the doing, and it needn't be tomorrow.
- **Set up tomorrow's runway, not just tomorrow's block.** Name the first thing *and* ask what needs to be ready for it — file open, notes found, on the desk and ideally alone there. That's where a good morning usually leaks away.
- **Keep it to five minutes, and don't coach.** A close that feels like more work doesn't happen at 6pm. Whatever you noticed will still be true tomorrow.
